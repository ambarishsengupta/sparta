Sparta: how the pilot works, screen by screen and row by row

Sparta is a free digital wardrobe for men. A user's own items feed outfit suggestions for that user. Their anonymised, aggregated data feeds the gap and demand insights that help a brand or retailer plan its range and stock. This page sketches that flow, runs the outfit rules on an example wardrobe, and shows the public data the team works from before the pilot has data of its own.

Wireframe only: boxes stand in for real UI. Anything tagged example is made-up sample data. Figures in section 3 are the real D2 audit numbers.

Section 3

The data behind it

The pilot has no user data yet. Until it does, the team works with two public datasets. H&M purchase records stand in for purchase behaviour and seasonal demand. Fashion-MNIST teaches the small garment model to recognise a garment type. Neither one shows ownership, wishlists, or which clothes go well together.

Fashion-MNIST: what the garment model learns

Each picture is 28 × 28 greyscale. There are 6,000 training and 1,000 test pictures per type. The team's target is about 90% accuracy on the 10,000 test pictures. The model only suggests a category when an item is added by photo. It never decides which clothes go together.
