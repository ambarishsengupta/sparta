Sparta process flow: outfit recommendation and inventory planning

ANCP Group M. One flow for the pilot: a user's wardrobe feeds a recommendation engine (rules and ranking) for that user and, as anonymised aggregates only, feeds the vendor insights that support range and stock decisions.
A. Consumer: outfit recommendation (rules and ranking, one user, own data only)
Pilot users are invited adults who accepted the first-login notice. No model is trained on participant data and no other user's data is used to build a suggestion.
B. Consent and anonymised aggregation (the only path from users to vendors)
Runs nightly in the pilot. Vendors never see user-level rows, photos, receipts, names, emails or free-text notes.
C. Vendor: inventory and range planning (aggregates only)
The primary decision is product design and range planning. Inventory and stocking by category is the secondary decision.
A1 Add what you own

Four entry routes: manual form; photo upload with background removal and a suggested category from the garment model (E), which the user confirms; receipt scan (OCR); catalogue or barcode lookup.

Seed catalogue from public storefront data supports quick add

A2 Wardrobe, purchases, wishlist

Items use the fixed taxonomy: category, colour (RGB, with derived colour family), fit, size, brand

Condition 0 to 100; purchases with receipts; wishlist

A3 Pick occasion and season

Occasions: Casual, Work, Formal, Party, Sport, Date, Travel

Season comes from the date and the user's hemisphere (R2)

Date and Travel use the Casual rule for now

A4 Rules R1 to R8

Hard: R1 occasion, R2 season; a top, a bottom and footwear; no item used twice

Scored: R3 colour harmony (at most 3 non-neutral families, 1 bold colour), R4 one pattern, R5 layering, R6 footwear and belt

A5 Rank and show up to three

Score: rule fit, occasion fit, completeness, condition, favourites. Same inputs, same suggestions

Each shows a one-line reason (R8); R7 keeps the three varied

If the wardrobe is too thin it says what is missing

A6 Accept, swap or reject

Accept saves the outfit

Swap lists valid items, most similar first

Accepts and rejects are counted (no item recorded) so the team can tune rules by hand

B1 Pilot notice accepted

First login: user must accept the notice to join the study

Sharing is on and locked during the pilot

Leave the pilot deletes the account and data

B2 Anonymised copy

Only consenting, active users

No names, emails, images, receipts, free text or shape features

Colour family replaces raw RGB

B3 Segment counts

Segment = category, colour family, fit, brand, size...

Distinct owning users and wishlist users, purchases by month, condition bands

B4 Privacy rules

Minimum group of 10 users (50 in production)

Counts rounded to the nearest 5; complementary suppression; one filter at a time

B5 Aggregate tables

Gap score = wishlist users / owning users

Run log: date, users, items, suppressed segments

B6 Admin review

Monthly report checked before a vendor sees it

Vendor query log; 200 calls per hour limit

C1 Vendor portal (read only)

Ownership mix by category, colour family, fit, brand

Purchase trend by month; wishlist trend; condition spectrum

Filters: size, season, price band or region, one at a time

C2 Gaps and demand

Gaps: high wishlist demand, low ownership, ranked by gap score

Condition spectrum shows replacement demand

Monthly gap and demand report

C3 Range design decision

Primary: which categories, colours and fits to add or expand next season

Decision-maker: head of product and design

C4 Inventory and stock decision

Secondary: how much to commit by category

Category-level demand and condition signals

Decision-maker: inventory and merchandising manager

C5 Vendor feedback

Pilot proof: the vendor says whether the dashboard or report would change a range or stock decision

Documented feedback

C6 Product feed upload

Vendor uploads its catalogue (attributes, price)

Reviewed by the admin, then joins the seed catalogue that A1 quick add uses

D Course analytics (ANCP D2 to D5): validates the vendor insights

Public H&M transaction data (proxy for purchase behaviour and seasonality, adults, cleaned in D2) plus aggregated pilot figures built under the same privacy rules. No row about an individual participant is used.

Outputs: gap ranking checked on a time-based holdout (precision@10 against a most-popular baseline), seasonal demand forecast (WAPE against last year), decision brief.

E Built before the pilot (offline): garment model

Fashion-MNIST (70,000 public garment pictures, MIT licence) trains a small image model once; it is tested on photos of team-owned clothes. It suggests a category for photo items in A1 and gives swap choices a sense of shape (A6).

Never trained or tuned on participant data. Outfit compatibility comes from the style specialist's rules, not from this data.

Legend and rules that apply everywhere

Solid arrow: data or control flow. Dashed: feedback, review or offline work.

Blue: consumer side. Amber: consent and aggregation. Green: vendor side. Violet: course analytics. Teal: built before the pilot.

No credentials, personal data or raw H&M or Fashion-MNIST files are stored in the public repository.
