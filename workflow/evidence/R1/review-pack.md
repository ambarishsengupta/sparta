## What the style reviewer checks

Read every row as a man looking at the outfit on a bed. Mark the last column `Y` (acceptable) or `N` (not), with a short note for each N. Then report acceptable / total.

1. **Occasion.** Does every piece suit the occasion (a hard rule, R1)? Work: shirt or polo, chinos or trousers, closed shoes. Formal: shirt, tailored trousers or suit, formal shoes. Party: dark or bold top, jeans or trousers, sneakers or boots. Sport: activewear and sneakers. Casual, Date and Travel share the Casual rule (no formal shoes with shorts).
2. **Season.** Nothing too heavy in summer (no coats, sweaters, cardigans) and nothing in the wrong season tag (R2). In autumn and winter, and for Formal, there must be a layer wherever the wardrobe has one (decision D-A); R5 scores the layer.
3. **Colour.** Neutrals with at most one bold item, no more than three colour families (R3). Flag clashing colours the rule cannot see.
4. **Pattern.** At most one patterned item (R4). Flag two busy patterns that slip through (for example stripes with a print).
5. **Shoes and belt.** Belt and shoes in the same colour family (R6). Flag shoes that are technically allowed but wrong (sandals with a blazer, running shoes with trousers).
6. **Variety.** The up-to-three suggestions for one occasion and season should differ, not be one outfit with a sock swapped (R7).
7. **Reasons.** Each reason must be true for the outfit and read naturally (R8).
8. **Score parts.** rule_fit, occasion_fit, completeness, condition and favourites are each 0 to 1; the score is 0.50, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10, 0.10 of them. Flag any outfit whose score looks wrong against how good it looks.

Draft data note: the penalty sizes, the neutral colour list and the sub-type choices behind the rules are drafts owned by the style specialist; findings here feed those values.
